About the Festival

What is Holi?

Holi is the ancient Hindu festival of colors, celebrated on the full moon of Phalguna (February–March). It heralds the arrival of spring, the triumph of good over evil, and the joy of togetherness — with vibrant powdered colors, water guns, music, and sweets.

🌸

Spring Welcome

Holi marks the end of winter and the glorious arrival of spring — a season of bloom and renewal.

🔥

Holika Dahan

The night before, bonfires are lit celebrating the victory of devotion over arrogance — Holika Dahan.

🎨

Festival of Colors

People smear each other with gulal (colored powder) and drench one another with colored water.

🍬

Food & Sweets

Gujiya, thandai, puran poli — Holi is incomplete without its iconic festive delicacies.

The Colors of Holi

Each color in Holi carries a deep symbolic meaning rooted in Indian tradition.

Pink — Love
Yellow — Prosperity
Green — Harmony
Blue — Divinity
Orange — Energy
Purple — Magic
Red — Passion
How We Celebrate

Holi Traditions

From ancient rituals to modern street parties, discover the heart of Holi.

🔥

Holika Dahan

Communities gather around sacred bonfires the night before to pray and celebrate good's victory over evil.

🎨

Rang Panchami

On the main day, streets turn into rivers of color as people chase each other with gulal and pichkaris.

🎵

Music & Dance

Dhol beats and folk songs fill the air as communities dance together in colorful euphoria.

🍹

Thandai & Sweets

Spiced thandai milk, gujiya dumplings, and puran poli complete the festive feast with loved ones.
